Teaching & mentoring

Under what conditions could this work?

One of my favorite questions in circuits lab was why we used two resistors to get an intermediate voltage from a 5 V supply. Once we worked through the calculation, we could ask what would happen to the output when we connected a load.

That is what I want students to take from a course. Start with the physical question, explain the equation in your own words, and use it to predict what happens when something changes.

Teaching experience

Circuits and solid-state devices at UT Austin

I spent four years as a teaching assistant for Fundamentals of Electronic Circuits I Lab (ECE 438) and Solid-State Electronic Devices (ECE 339). I helped students troubleshoot circuits at the bench, used LTspice in circuit labs, and developed solid-state device assignments with nanoHUB.

For three of those years, I coordinated undergraduate teaching assistants. Their experience as former students helped me see where instructions needed clarification. I revised lab-report guidelines that later sections adopted.

Applied physics labs at NUST

I began teaching with a year as a laboratory demonstrator at the National University of Sciences and Technology in Pakistan. In Applied Physics 301 and 302, I worked with students from physics, engineering, and chemistry on the same laboratory problems.

Mentoring students in research

When a student is stuck, I ask them to walk me through what they tried and what they expected. I have also helped undergraduate researchers prepare posters and practice explaining a result to someone outside their field.

Courses I’d like to develop

Two-Dimensional Materials for Emerging Technologies

Students would compare memory and energy-storage materials, tracing differences in device behavior back to composition, defects, and interfaces.

Computational Techniques for Electronic Materials

Students would pose a materials question, choose an atomistic model, check its assumptions, and compare their prediction with experimental evidence.
