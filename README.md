# Jameela Fatheema, PhD

I am a computational materials researcher working at the intersection of
two-dimensional materials, semiconductor devices, and nonvolatile memory.
I earned my PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering from The University
of Texas at Austin in 2026.

My work uses first-principles calculations to understand atomic-scale
switching: how metal atoms move through defects, how interfaces change
migration barriers, and how charge redistribution relates to device behavior.

## Research focus

- **2D materials and defects:** MoS₂, h-BN, and In₂Se₃ for emerging memory devices.
- **Electronic structure and atomistic modeling:** DFT, hybrid functionals,
  CI-NEB, ab initio molecular dynamics, Bader charge analysis, and DFT-NEGF.
- **Reproducible research workflows:** Python and Bash automation for VASP
  calculations, data processing, visualization, and HPC job management.

## Selected work

- **[Bader charge analysis for metal/h-BN switching paths](https://github.com/j-shah-7/Bader-Charge):**
  prepares charge-density calculations and summarizes atom-resolved charge
  transfer across structures and migration images. The repository includes
  an analysis-only synthetic example.
- **[First-principles investigation of resistive switching in monolayer MoS₂](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41699-025-00593-x):**
  published in *npj 2D Materials and Applications* (2025).

I am developing additional computational workflows for metal/h-BN interfaces
and defect-mediated switching. My current interests include multilayer h-BN
defects, machine-learning-assisted atomistic dynamics, and materials screening
for memory and spintronic devices.

**Tools:** VASP · Quantum ESPRESSO · QuantumATK · Python · Bash · Linux · SLURM · MPI
